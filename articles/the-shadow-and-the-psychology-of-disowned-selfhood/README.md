# The Shadow and the Psychology of Disowned Selfhood

This companion scaffold supports the article **“The Shadow and the Psychology of Disowned Selfhood”** in the Analytical Psychology, Symbolism & the Depth Mind knowledge series.

The repository materials translate the article’s argument into reproducible, synthetic-data workflows. The goal is not to diagnose people, interpret real dreams, evaluate mental health, rank morality, automate Jungian interpretation, or reduce shadow dynamics to a literal computational object. The goal is to demonstrate how latent disowned material, ego identity, persona rigidity, cue intensity, affective charge, reflective capacity, shadow discrepancy, shadow activation, projection intensity, shame response, defensiveness, withdrawal, responsibility, and integration capacity can be represented transparently in conceptual modeling.

## Repository purpose

This article argues that the shadow is the dimension of the psyche that the conscious personality refuses, disowns, represses, or cannot reconcile with its preferred image of itself. The shadow is not simply evil. It is the rejected remainder of the personality: aggression, envy, dependency, grief, vulnerability, desire, ambition, creativity, tenderness, truthfulness, or any capacity that conscious identity cannot yet include.

The code examples extend that argument through:

- synthetic shadow-projection data;
- persona-rigidity and ego-identity modeling;
- reflective-capacity workflows;
- shadow-discrepancy simulation;
- projection, shame, defensiveness, and withdrawal indices;
- disowned-selfhood network modeling;
- integration and responsibility metrics;
- collective-shadow extension notes;
- SQL schemas for structured shadow-dynamics examples;
- multi-language computational scaffolding;
- documented responsible-use limits.

## Responsible-use statement

These materials are for synthetic-data research, methods demonstration, conceptual modeling, symbolic-process analysis, institutional learning, and reproducible workflows. They are not intended for diagnosis, therapy, psychological assessment, clinical decision-making, treatment recommendation, mental-health evaluation, crisis intervention, moral ranking, employment screening, workplace surveillance, individual performance management, or individual evaluation.

## Suggested workflow

1. Review `article-metadata.yml` for article context.
2. Inspect `data/raw/synthetic_shadow_projection_panel.csv`.
3. Inspect `data/raw/shadow_network_nodes.csv` and `data/raw/shadow_network_edges.csv`.
4. Run the R workflow in `r/model_shadow_projection_dynamics.R`.
5. Run the Python workflow in `python/model_shadow_activation_network.py`.
6. Use `sql/schema.sql` to inspect the shadow-dynamics schema.
7. Treat all outputs as illustrative and synthetic, not clinical findings, moral evaluations, psychological assessments, predictions, or proof of Jungian theory.
