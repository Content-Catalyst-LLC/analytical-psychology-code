# Method Notes

This scaffold uses synthetic conceptual variables to illustrate the article’s argument. The variables are not validated psychological, clinical, therapeutic, or outcome measures and should not be interpreted as measurements of real patients, therapists, sessions, treatment effects, or clinical effectiveness.

## Modeling logic

The article’s core claim is that analytical psychology in clinical practice supports change by strengthening ego integration, symbolic capacity, relational safety, affect regulation, dream richness, and shadow awareness while reducing symptom burden, complex activation, conscious one-sidedness, trauma fragmentation, shame load, and compensatory pressure.

A simplified clinical-functioning model is:

```text
clinical functioning =
  ego integration
  + symbolic capacity
  + relational safety
  + affect regulation
  + shadow awareness
  - symptom burden
  - compensatory pressure
  - trauma fragmentation
  - shame load
```

A simplified compensatory-pressure model is:

```text
compensatory pressure =
  conscious one-sidedness
  + complex activation
  + trauma fragmentation
  - ego integration
  - shadow awareness
```

A simplified symbolic-capacity model is:

```text
symbolic capacity =
  relational safety
  + affect regulation
  + ego integration
  + dream richness
  - trauma fragmentation
  - shame load
```

## Limits

These examples are conceptual demonstrations. They should not be used for:

- diagnosis;
- therapy;
- psychological assessment;
- treatment recommendation;
- therapist evaluation;
- clinical decision-making;
- employee screening;
- workplace surveillance;
- individual scoring;
- clinical triage;
- claims about empirical effectiveness;
- proof or disproof of Jungian psychotherapy.

## Recommended use

Use these files to demonstrate reproducible research structure, transparent assumptions, synthetic simulation, dynamic treatment-network modeling, and conceptual-method documentation in scholarly writing.
