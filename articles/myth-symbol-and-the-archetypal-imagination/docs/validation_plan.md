# Validation Plan

This scaffold is conceptual and synthetic. A serious empirical, literary, historical, theological, anthropological, or political analysis would require careful validation before making claims about myth, symbol, archetypes, religion, ritual, political myth, or cultural memory.

## Possible validation steps

1. Define each motif category clearly.
2. Separate conceptual modeling, close reading, historical analysis, and empirical corpus analysis.
3. Use public-domain, licensed, or ethically approved texts.
4. Avoid restricted sacred, ceremonial, private, clinical, dream-journal, or community-held materials unless permission and expertise are present.
5. Work with relevant domain experts: historians, theologians, anthropologists, literary scholars, religious-studies scholars, political theorists, linguists, and community knowledge holders.
6. Include original languages and translation issues where relevant.
7. Compare alternative explanations for recurrence, such as genre convention, borrowing, colonial transmission, ecology, shared political conditions, or editorial selection.
8. Document motif-dictionary choices and coding decisions.
9. Treat network outputs as exploratory maps.
10. Pair computational findings with close reading and historical context.

## Non-goals

This scaffold does not prove archetypes, automate myth interpretation, classify religions, rank cultures, diagnose communities, interpret private dreams, validate Jungian theory, or replace scholarly judgment.
