# Method Notes

This scaffold uses synthetic conceptual variables to illustrate the article’s argument. The variables are not validated measures of Jung’s corpus, historical influence, clinical truth, interpretive authority, or concept priority.

## Modeling logic

The article’s core claim is that analytical psychology formed historically through clinical work, psychiatric research, Freud, complex theory, symbolic expansion, comparative inquiry, inward exploration, and post-Jungian revision.

A simplified intellectual-formation model is:

```text
Jungian formation =
  psychiatric-clinical experience
  + Freudian influence
  + complex research
  + comparative symbolic inquiry
  + inward exploration
  + institutional development
  + post-Jungian revision
```

A simplified network model is:

```text
conceptual importance =
  node centrality
  + phase activation
  + domain relevance
  + historical interpretive support
```

A simplified responsible-interpretation model is:

```text
interpretive confidence =
  textual evidence
  + historical context
  + concept recurrence
  + scholarly corroboration
  - overgeneralization risk
  - cultural flattening risk
  - retrospective simplification risk
```

## Interpretive safeguards

- Concept centrality is not proof of historical importance.
- Synthetic networks clarify assumptions; they do not replace close reading.
- Jungian concepts require historical, textual, institutional, biographical, cultural, and clinical evidence.
- Symbolic recurrence is not the same as universal meaning.
- Post-Jungian revision is part of the tradition, not an external afterthought.
- Gender-symbolic concepts require critical revision.
- Cultural materials should not be flattened into psychological evidence without context.
- Mathematical or network models should support interpretation, not replace it.

Use these files to demonstrate reproducible research structure, transparent assumptions, intellectual-history modeling, concept-network analysis, phase comparison, and responsible documentation.
