# Validation Plan

This scaffold is conceptual and synthetic. A serious scholarly extension would require careful validation before making any claim about Jung’s intellectual development, concept priority, historical causation, institutional formation, or post-Jungian influence.

## Possible validation steps

1. Define concepts before coding.
2. Specify which works, letters, lectures, or secondary sources are included.
3. Separate Jung’s early, middle, late, and post-Jungian phases with transparent criteria.
4. Code concept co-occurrence from actual texts rather than synthetic examples.
5. Preserve textual context rather than relying only on word frequency.
6. Include rival interpretations from Freud studies, history of psychiatry, religious studies, feminist scholarship, cultural studies, and intellectual history.
7. Compare network results with close reading.
8. Track changes in concept meaning across time, not just term occurrence.
9. Distinguish Jung’s own claims from later post-Jungian reinterpretations.
10. Treat network outputs as aids to interpretation, not as proof.

## Non-goals

This scaffold does not prove analytical psychology, validate Jung’s metaphysics, diagnose people, assess personality, rank concepts by truth, replace scholarship, or settle controversies in Jung studies. It is an educational and conceptual modeling scaffold.
