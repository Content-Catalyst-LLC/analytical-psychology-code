# Method Notes

This scaffold uses synthetic conceptual variables to illustrate the article’s epistemological argument. The variables are not validated psychological scales and should not be interpreted as measurements of individuals, clinicians, patients, or real clinical evidence.

## Modeling logic

The article’s core claim is that analytical psychology becomes more credible when different kinds of claim are paired with appropriate standards of warrant.

A simplified credibility model is:

```text
credibility =
  empirical support
  + hermeneutic coherence
  + clinical utility
  + phenomenological adequacy
  + contextual specificity
  + methodological explicitness
  - ambiguity inflation
```

A simplified overreach-risk model is:

```text
overreach risk =
  universalizing force
  + ambiguity inflation
  + selective evidence
  - methodological explicitness
  - contextual specificity
```

## Limits

These examples are conceptual demonstrations. They should not be used for:

- diagnosis;
- therapy;
- psychological assessment;
- employee screening;
- workplace surveillance;
- individual scoring;
- clinical triage;
- claims about empirical effectiveness;
- proof or disproof of Jungian theory.

## Recommended use

Use these files to demonstrate reproducible research structure, transparent assumptions, synthetic simulation, epistemic-network modeling, and conceptual-method documentation in scholarly writing.
