# Method Notes

This scaffold uses synthetic conceptual variables to illustrate the article’s argument. The variables are not validated psychological, clinical, therapeutic, life-outcome, or assessment measures and should not be interpreted as measurements of real people or predictions about actual midlife development.

## Modeling logic

The article’s core claim is that midlife becomes psychologically significant when first-half structures of persona, adaptation, achievement, and social recognition no longer provide sufficient meaning coherence. The transition intensifies when outward-inward discrepancy, unlived-life pressure, and finitude awareness rise. Individuation potential increases when symbolic activation, shadow integration, and reflective capacity strengthen enough to reorganize the personality around a deeper center.

A simplified meaning-coherence model is:

```text
meaning coherence =
  adaptation strength
  + symbolic activation
  + individuation pressure
  + shadow integration
  + reflective capacity
  - outward-inward discrepancy
  - social constraint
```

A simplified transition-intensity model is:

```text
transition intensity =
  outward-inward discrepancy
  + unlived-life pressure
  + finitude awareness
  + individuation pressure
  - reflective capacity
```

A simplified second-half-orientation model is:

```text
second-half orientation =
  symbolic activation
  + shadow integration
  + reflective capacity
  + finitude awareness
  - persona identification
  - outward-inward discrepancy
```

## Limits

These examples are conceptual demonstrations. They should not be used for:

- diagnosis;
- therapy;
- psychological assessment;
- life prediction;
- treatment recommendation;
- clinical decision-making;
- employment screening;
- workplace surveillance;
- individual scoring;
- individual evaluation;
- advice about whether to leave a job, relationship, or life situation;
- claims about empirical effectiveness;
- proof or disproof of Jungian midlife theory.

## Recommended use

Use these files to demonstrate reproducible research structure, transparent assumptions, synthetic simulation, dynamic reorientation-network modeling, and conceptual-method documentation in scholarly writing.
