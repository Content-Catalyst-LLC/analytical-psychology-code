# Trauma and Dissociation Discernment Checklist

Use this checklist for conceptual and editorial review. It is not a treatment protocol.

## Safety and scope

- Has acute risk been assessed before symbolic interpretation?
- Are suicidality, self-harm, psychosis, mania, substance use, domestic violence, or medical instability present?
- Does the person need crisis care, psychiatric consultation, medical evaluation, or specialized trauma support?
- Is symbolic work appropriate for the person’s current regulatory capacity?

## Dissociation and fragmentation

- Is the material symbolic, dissociative, intrusive, or a mixture?
- Are affect, body, memory, and narrative disconnected?
- Is the person present enough to use interpretation?
- Are self-states being respected rather than forced into premature unity?

## Symbolic capacity

- Does dream work deepen integration or increase fragmentation?
- Is amplification helping or bypassing the body and affect?
- Is active imagination safe, or could it intensify dissociation?
- Is symbolic deadness being respected as a protective adaptation?

## Moral clarity

- Is trauma being confused with shadow?
- Is the survivor being blamed, subtly or directly, for what happened?
- Is concrete harm being acknowledged before symbolic meaning is explored?
- Are perpetrator, institutional, social, or historical dynamics being named where relevant?

## Integration

- Are body, affect, memory, symbol, and relationship becoming more connected?
- Is the work paced slowly enough for safety?
- Is witness present before meaning is demanded?
- Is the wound being respected without romanticizing it?
