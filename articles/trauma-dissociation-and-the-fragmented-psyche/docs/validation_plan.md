# Validation Plan

This scaffold is conceptual and synthetic. A serious empirical or clinical extension would require careful validation before making any claim about trauma, dissociation, symbolic recovery, treatment, clinical outcomes, diagnosis, or risk.

## Possible validation steps

1. Define each construct clearly.
2. Separate clinical interpretation, research measurement, and conceptual modeling.
3. Use validated trauma, dissociation, symptom, functioning, and psychotherapy-process measures where appropriate.
4. Work with qualified trauma clinicians and ethics review processes for human-subjects research.
5. Avoid individual-level scoring unless a validated instrument exists for that purpose.
6. Protect privacy and never use identifiable clinical or trauma material without consent and ethical approval.
7. Include patient, therapist, cultural, developmental, institutional, and social context when interpreting data.
8. Include disconfirming cases and alternative explanations.
9. Document uncertainty and avoid overclaiming.
10. Treat model outputs as hypotheses or demonstrations, not clinical truth.

## Non-goals

This scaffold does not validate Jungian trauma theory, diagnose dissociation, recommend treatment, assess risk, evaluate patients, predict outcomes, or replace clinical judgment. It is an educational and conceptual modeling scaffold.
