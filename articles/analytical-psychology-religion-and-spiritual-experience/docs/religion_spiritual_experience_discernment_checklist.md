# Religion and Spiritual Experience Discernment Checklist

Use this checklist when thinking conceptually about analytical psychology, religion, and spiritual experience.

## Symbol and doctrine

- Is the religious symbol alive, or has it hardened into dead literalism?
- Does doctrine mediate mystery or defend against it?
- Does the symbol bear contradiction, or does it close interpretation too quickly?
- Does the religious form contain numinous intensity, or intensify fear and projection?

## Ritual and containment

- What ritual, liturgical, communal, or embodied container is present?
- Does the practice support humility, responsibility, and integration?
- Does it help regulate grief, guilt, longing, dependence, and awe?
- Does it become coercive, empty, shame-based, or compulsive?

## Shadow and ethics

- Does religion deepen shadow awareness or project darkness onto outsiders?
- Does spiritual language increase accountability or moral exemption?
- Are aggression, envy, dependency, shame, sexuality, and power being examined?
- Does the religious community protect vulnerable people and restrain authority?

## Spiritual experience

- Does the experience increase humility, compassion, and symbolic flexibility?
- Does it produce inflation, specialness, apocalyptic urgency, or private authority?
- Can the experience be held metaphorically and tested over time?
- Does it strengthen ordinary life or detach the person from responsibility?

## Clinical and cultural humility

- Could trauma, dissociation, mania, grief, or religious abuse be involved?
- What tradition-specific meanings are being engaged?
- Who has legitimate interpretive authority in that tradition?
- Is psychological language illuminating or appropriating religious meaning?
